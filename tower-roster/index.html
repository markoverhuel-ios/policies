Tower Roster

RF limit check for tower climbers

OverviewPrivacySupport

Know what you are climbing before you climb. Tower Roster looks up a tower by its ASR number, lists every licensed radio on it by company, and checks an RF meter reading against the FCC exposure limit. It uses public FCC license and ASR data, updated weekly.

Features

  • Look up a tower by ASR number, or find registered towers near you
  • Tower owner, height, and a link to the FCC's own record
  • Every licensed radio on the tower, grouped by company
  • Calculated share of the FCC limit for each radio at your working distance, with the companies over the limit listed
  • Phone numbers for the tower owner, each license's control point, and the licensee, with one-tap calling (business and agency numbers only)
  • A ready-made shutdown message listing the companies and radios, to send by text or email
  • RF meter check: enter a reading, compare it to the FCC limit, see the time allowed, and keep a log per tower
  • FCC exposure limits reference, light and dark mode, and offline use for areas you have looked up

Tower Roster is an aid, not a safety clearance. It is not a lockout plan or an RF survey. FCC and FAA records can be wrong, out of date, or incomplete. Cell carriers are licensed by area, so most of their equipment is not in the records. Verify everything with the tower owner and on site.

How it works

  1. Choose how close you will get to antennas and whether you have RF safety training.
  2. Enter the tower's ASR number, or list towers near you.
  3. The app shows who is licensed on the tower and which radios exceed the FCC limit at your distance.
  4. Call the owner or licensee, or send the shutdown message.
  5. Enter your meter reading to compare it with the limit and start the timer.

Who it is for

Tower climbing crews, crew leads, and RF safety staff.
